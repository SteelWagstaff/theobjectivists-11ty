Lorine Faith Niedecker was born on May 11, 1903 in the Summer House, a modest two-story home located near the Rock River on Blackhawk Island, Wisconsin, which her parents, Henry Niedecker and Theresa "Daisy" Kunz, had purchased from Daisy's parents shortly before Lorine was born.<sup id="fnref-1"><a href="#fn-1">[1]</a></sup>

## Parents and Ancestry

### Niedecker Ancestors

Lorine's father, Henry Niedecker was born in Sutter, California in 1879 to Charles <sup id="fnref-2"><a href="#fn-2">[2]</a></sup> and Ameila (Emelia) Schneider \[his father remarried Maria Margaretha Brandel in 1883\]. The youngest of four children (his older siblings were George August,<sup id="fnref-3"><a href="#fn-3">[3]</a></sup> Charles Jacob,<sup id="fnref-4"><a href="#fn-4">[4]</a></sup> and Emma<sup id="fnref-5"><a href="#fn-5">[5]</a></sup>) Henry's early childhood was massively disrupted by his mother's death shortly after his birth and the flooding of the family farm near Sacramento. In 1883, Charles, recently widowed and newly landless, returned to Wisconsin with his four children, settling on a farm near Fort Atkinson and marrying Mary Brandel, a German immigrant who had no children of her own, in the following year.<sup id="fnref-6"><a href="#fn-6">[6]</a></sup>

Henry was an outgoing, enterprising man, and while he was well liked by many neighbors, he was plagued throughout his life by his profligate spending and poor financial judgment.

### Kunz Ancestors

Theresa Henrietta "Daisy" Kunz, Lorine's mother, was born on Blackhawk Island in 1879, the youngest of two children.<sup id="fnref-7"><a href="#fn-7">[7]</a></sup> Her parents, Gottfried Karl (Charles) Kunz (1811-1883) and Louise Amelia Schlosser \[Schlvaser?\] (1840-?), owned and operated the Fountain House, a ten-guest hotel, resort, bar, and restaurant on Blackhawk Island. Charles' parents, George Philip Kunz (1811-1883) and Caroline Zeasar (1817-1880), were Prussian immigrants, and it was Caroline who first purchased land in the Blackhawk Island region in 1857.

Henry and Daisy were school mates, attending the Black Hawk Country school, and married on August 28, 1901 at St. John's Community Church in Oakland, Wisconsin.<sup id="fnref-8"><a href="#fn-8">[8]</a></sup>

## First Marriage

On November 29, 1928, Niedecker married Frank Hartwig, a road contractor she had known since her high school days in Fort Atkinson. Their wedding, conducted in the parsonage of St. Paul's Lutheran Church, an attractive cream-colored brick building near downtown Fort Atkinson, was a quiet, modest affair; Frank apparently only told his brother Ernest, who served as best man, that they were marrying the day before the ceremony, and the couple did not hold a public reception of any kind. Hartwig's parents were German immigrants who raised Holstein cows on a thirty-acre farm just a few miles from the Niedeckers, and after their marriage, the new couple moved into a home that Hartwig had recently built on Garfield Street in Fort Atkinson. Their marriage, which did not appear at first glance to be a good fit, proved to be short lived, probably doomed by a combination of sexual, economic, and emotional stressors.<sup id="fnref-9"><a href="#fn-9">[9]</a></sup> The Great Depression began with the collapse of the stock market in October 1929, wiping out Hartwig's construction business (he had purchased much of his equipment on credit) and causing him to default on their home payments. Hartwig's father also died in 1929, and having lost his business and their home, Frank moved back to the family farm to help his mother continue dairy operations. Niedecker did not; while she and Hartwig apparently remained friendly, she chose instead to live separately, splitting her time over the next few years between her parent's home on Blackhawk Island and living with her cousins, Roland and Maude Hartel, in town.<sup id="fnref-10"><a href="#fn-10">[10]</a></sup>

The location was not far from the current location of the Island Bar & Grill. <a href="#fnref-1">↩</a></p>

<p id="fn-2">2. Charles Niedecker was born in Alsace-Lorraine in 1840 to George and Barbara Niedecker and emigrated to Wisconsin with his parents sometime in the mid-1840s. The oldest of six children, the 1870 census records him as working as a farm laborer in his parents' household in Oakland, Wisconsin, along with his wife, Amelia, then 18 years old, and their infant son, George (then just six months old). The 1880 census records the family, minus Emelia, as living in Sutter, California, near Sacramento. ??Charles Niedecker died in 1921 in Beloit, Wisconsin.?? <a href="#fnref-2">↩</a></p>

<p id="fn-3">3. George August was born in Wisconsin in 1870, and it was shortly after his birth that his parents moved to California to establish their own farm. George married Emma Louise Ehlers (the younger sister of Minnie Ehlers, the woman his younger brother Charles Jr. had married the year before) in Jefferson County on October 6, 1897. George and Emma had two daughters: Esther (born in Sumer, Wisconsin on July 8, 1900) and Edna Barbra (born in Fort Atkinson on June 1, 1905). The 1910 census records the family as living on Adams Street in Fort Atkinson, where George worked as a 'retail merchant'. The 1920 census records George as working as a carpenter and the family as living on Madison Avenue in Fort Atkinson (then Koshkonong Township). The 1940 census gives George and Emma as living at 1220 Maple Avenue in Janesville, Wisconsin along with their widowed daughter Esther Glover, and her two daughters Mary (aged 13) and Helen (aged 12). George, then over 70, is listed as working as a janitor in an automobile factory, and it appears that they also rented a part of their home to a younger married couple for additional income. Emma Niedecker died in 1943, Esther Neidecker Glover died in 1944, and George died in 1949. All three were buried in the Evergreen Cemetery in Fort Atkinson, not far from Minnie Ehlers Neidecker's grave site. <a href="#fnref-3">↩</a></p>

<p id="fn-4">4. Charles Jacob Niedecker was born in California in 1871. He married Minnie Ehlers (the older sister of Emma Ehlers, who would marry George August Niedecker in the following year) in Jefferson County on May 13, 1896, and their only son Lawrence Henry was born in Lake Mills, Wisconsin on December 20, 1896. The 1900 Census records Charles and Minnie living on N. Main Street in Fort Atkinson where Charles gave his occupation as a saloon keeper. Shortly thereafter, the family moved to Beloit, Wisconsin where they lived for three years before moving briefly to a farm in Shirland, Illinois, where Minnie became ill and died on September 3, 1904 at just 32 years of age. Minnie came from a large, well-known Fort Atkinson family and her funeral, held at the Congregational church in Fort Atkinson, was attended by more well-wishers than the building could hold. Following Minnie's death, Charles remarried (a woman named Ellen), and by 1910, the family had moved to 123 Moore Street in Beloit, Wisconsin, where Charles worked as a bartender in a local saloon. The 1920 census records the family as living at 1216 Vine Street in Beloit, where 'Charley' worked as a 'tinner' in a woodworking shop and in 1930 Charles and Ellen were living in Roscoe, Illinois, where Charles worked as a sheet metal worker in a tin shop. Ellen most likely died some time between 1930 and 1940, since the 1940 census records Charles as a single lodger staying in the home of Mary Armfield on Merrill St. in Beloit, Wisconsin, where he worked as a sheet metal worker in a wood working machine factory. Charles died in 1964 and was buried in the Niedecker family plot at Union Cemetery, just west of Fort Atkinson. Charles and Minnie's only son Lawrence made his living as a commercial photographer in Chicago (according to his WWII draft registration card, he was working for the Berlet Anderson Marlin advertising firm in downtown Chicago in 1942). Lawrence married a woman named Mae in 1924 and his wife had a son, John, from a previous relationship. In 1930, the family lived at 3745 Sheffield Avenue, less than a block north of Wrigley Field, and were living at 4837 North Damen Avenue in the Ravenswood/Lincoln Square neighborhood of Chicago when the census was conducted in 1940 and had purchased a home in Burton's Bridge, an unincorporated village 50 miles northwest of Chicago by 1942. Lawrence died in September 1975 and was buried in the Prairie Grove Cemetery in Illinois with his second wife, Mildred (born January 28, 1908, died September 1982). <a href="#fnref-4">↩</a></p>

<p id="fn-5">5. Emma was born in California in 1875 and married John Cormany in Rock County, Wisconsin on November 7, 1894 and the couple settled in Beloit, Wisconsin. Emma and John had four children: John H. (born December 9, 1895), Pearne (born December 11, 1896), Ada (born August 1, 1905), and Marie (born in 1908 or 1909). The 1910 census lists the Cormany family as living in Newark, Wisconsin (an unincorporated community 10 miles west of Beloit) but by 1920, the couple had returned to Beloit, where John worked as a carpenter and John Jr. as a laborer in a lumber yard. Pearne had left home by that time, but Emma's father Charles Sr., then 79 years old, had come to live with them, probably following his wife Mary's death. In 1930, John H. Cormany appears as an inmate in the county asylum in Janesville. The 1940 census shows Emma and John as still living at 1243 Eleventh St. in Beloit, and does not give an occupation for John, indicating that he was probably retired at this point. Emma's husband John died in 1948, Emma died on November 14, 1965, and John H. died in February 1981. All three were buried in Beloit's Eastlawn cemetery. <a href="#fnref-5">↩</a></p>
