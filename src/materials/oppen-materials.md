## Major Collections

The major collection of George and Mary Oppen’s papers are located at the Archive for New Poetry in the Mandeville Special Collections at the University of California, San Diego. Additional material is located at the Beinecke Library at Yale University, the Harry Ransom Center at the University of Texas-Austin, the University of Arizona, Indiana University, and the University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee.

### George Oppen Papers at Mandeville Special Collections, UCSD

> Materials range in date from 1958-1984 and include manuscripts and typescripts for all the poems contained in Oppen's nine published books, drafts and fragments of unpublished poems, typescripts of published and unpublished essays, transcripts of Oppen's verse, and copies of reviews of Oppen's work. Of special interest are loose leaf pages of notes, and Oppen's personal daybooks, all of which help to reveal his thinking about diverse subjects. The largest part of the collection consists of correspondence to Oppen from family members, editors, poets and other writers, and admirers of Oppen's work. A small addition, processed in 1989, includes correspondence, a 1973 interview transcript, reviews and ephemera.<sup id="fnref-1"><a href="#fn-1">[1]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

UCSD maintains a [detailed finding aid](https://library.ucsd.edu/speccoll/findingaids/mss0016.html) of the George Oppen papers.

### George Oppen Letters, 1930-1982 at Mandeville Special Collections, UCSD

> Research files compiled by Rachel Blau DuPlessis concerning American poet George Oppen, comprising photocopies of Oppen's letters (1930-1982), copies of Oppen's FBI file, and miscellaneous correspondence between DuPlessis and others pertaining to those Oppen materials. The results of DuPlessis's research were published in THE SELECTED LETTERS OF GEORGE OPPEN (1990). The addition processed in 1993 consists of letters from George Oppen to Rachel Blau DuPlessis from August 1965 to April 1977.<sup id="fnref-2"><a href="#fn-2">[2]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

UCSD maintains a [detailed finding aid](https://library.ucsd.edu/speccoll/findingaids/mss0016.html) of the George Oppen Letters.

### Mary Oppen Papers at Mandeville Special Collections, UCSD

> The Mary Oppen Papers contain extensive photographic documentation of the Oppen family, along with artworks by George and Mary Oppen and a small file of papers. The artworks include wood carvings by George Oppen and paintings and collages by Mary. Included in the papers are drafts of Mary Oppen's autobiography _Meaning A Life_, journals (including a dream journal), and original writings by her and her husband.<sup id="fnref-3"><a href="#fn-3">[3]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

UCSD maintains a [detailed finding aid](https://library.ucsd.edu/speccoll/findingaids/mss0125.html) of the Mary Oppen papers.

### June Oppen Degnan Papers at Mandeville Special Collections, UCSD

> Papers of June Oppen Degnan, writer, publisher, political activist, and sister of poet George Oppen (1908-1984). Most of the collection documents Degnan's activities as publisher of the San Francisco Review in the 1960s, and her association with New Directions Books. Included is correspondence and writings of many important members of the American literary community including Jack Anderson, Robert Bly, William Bronk, Basil Bunting, Hayden Carruth, Robert Creeley, Clayton Eshleman, Lawrence Ferlinghetti, Erich Fromm, Allen Ginsberg, David Ignatow, Denise Levertov, George Oppen, Carl Rakosi, Jerome Rothenberg, C. P. Snow, Gary Snyder, Diane Wakoski, William Carlos Williams, and Louis Zukofsky. Virtually no references to Degnan's personal or political life can be found in the papers. Of special significance are letters from George Oppen.<sup id="fnref-4"><a href="#fn-4">[4]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

UCSD maintains a [detailed finding aid](https://library.ucsd.edu/speccoll/findingaids/mss0017.html) of the June Oppen Degnan papers.

### Linda Oppen Mourelatos Correspondence, 1956-1982 at Mandeville Special Collections, UCSD

> Correspondence of Linda Oppen Mourelatos, daughter of the Objectivist poet George Oppen and his wife Mary. Of particular importance is the correspondence between Oppen and her parents; 3 folders of typescript poetry by George Oppen; and 8 folders of material by Mary Oppen, including 6 folders of a typescript for her autobiography MEANING A LIFE.<sup id="fnref-5"><a href="#fn-5">[5]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

UCSD maintains a [detailed finding aid](https://library.ucsd.edu/speccoll/findingaids/mss0017.html) of the Linda Oppen Mourelatos Correspondence collection.

## Letters at Yale University

### Ezra Pound Papers

Several letters from George Oppen to Ezra Pound are included in the Ezra Pound Papers collection held by the Beinecke Library at Yale University

#### Finding Aid

Yale University maintains [a detailed finding aid](http://drs.library.yale.edu/HLTransformer/HLTransServlet?stylename=yul.ead2002.xhtml.xsl&pid=beinecke:pound&query=George%20Oppen&clear-stylesheet-cache=yes&hlon=yes&big=y&adv=&filter=&hitPageStart=1&sortFields=&view=c01_1#c02.d0e688) for the Ezra Pound Papers. Oppen's letters to Pound can be found in Box 38, folders 1612-1613. An additional letter from Oppen sent to Pound in 1963 and the previously undiscovered manuscript that was published in 2017 as <a href="https://www.ndbooks.com/book/21-poems/"><em>21 Poems</em></a> can be found in the [Ezra Pound Papers Addition](http://drs.library.yale.edu/HLTransformer/HLTransServlet?stylename=yul.ead2002.xhtml.xsl&pid=beinecke:poundadd&query=George%20Oppen&clear-stylesheet-cache=yes&hlon=yes&big=&adv=&filter=&hitPageStart=1&sortFields=&view=all), Box 13, Folder 322 and Box 40, Folder 930, respectively.

## Letters at Stanford University

#### Michael Heller Papers

> The papers include correspondence dating from the early sixties with nearly 600 figures in the middle of the literary world including writers, critics, and publishers. Of particular note is correspondence with Cid Corman, Clayton Eshleman, Walter Hamady, Jackson Mac Low, George Oppen, Carl Rakosi, Armand Schwerner, John Taggart, Nathaniel Tarn, and Hannah Weiner. In addition there are files documenting Heller's work on George Oppen and Carl Rakosi, as well as notebooks, audio and videotapes, early manuscripts, literary ephemera and roughly 10,000 pages of manuscripts by other writers.<sup id="fnref-6"><a href="#fn-6">[6]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

The Online Archive of California maintains a [detailed finding aid](http://www.oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/kt787037m0/) for this collection.

#### The David Gitin Papers

> Primarily incoming letters (42) from George Oppen, 1968-1977, including Oppen manuscripts and photographs. Rough drafts of PRIMITIVE appear occasionally in the correspondence. There is a 39-page photocopy of a corrected typescript by Mary Oppen, a journal kept during the couple's trip to Israel.<sup id="fnref-7"><a href="#fn-7">[7]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

The Online Archive of California maintains a [detailed finding aid](http://www.oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/ft7v19n8w4/) for this collection.

### Letters at the University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee

#### The Martin J. Rosenblum Papers

> The collection consists largely of correspondence between Rosenblum and friends and colleagues, many of them poets and writers. This collection also contains the only final manuscript of Rosenblum's unpublished critical biography of Objectivist poet Carl Rakosi, Rosenblum's research materials relating to Rakosi and other Objectivist poets. Research materials on Rakosi include a recorded interview and rare photographs of Rakosi at his home both dating from the summer of 1974.<sup id="fnref-8"><a href="#fn-8">[8]</a></sup>

#### Finding Aid

The University of Wisconsin Digital Collections maintains [a detailed finding aid](http://digicoll.library.wisc.edu/cgi/f/findaid/findaid-idx?c=wiarchives;cc=wiarchives;view=text;rgn=main;didno=uw-mil-uwmmss0304) for this collection.
